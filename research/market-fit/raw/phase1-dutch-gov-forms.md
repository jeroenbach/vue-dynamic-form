# Phase 1 — Dutch government forms landscape (raw research notes)

Angle: Dutch government forms landscape — Open Formulieren, Common Ground, commercial "formulierenserver" vendors, procurement, DigiD, WCAG/accessibility obligations. Researched 2026-09-30.

## Market size / scoping facts

- Netherlands has **342 municipalities** (gemeenten) as of 1 Jan 2025, stable after decades of mergers (CBS). https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/onze-diensten/methoden/classificaties/overig/gemeentelijke-indelingen-per-jaar/indeling-per-jaar/gemeentelijke-indeling-op-1-januari-2025
- Plus 21 waterschappen (water boards, ~€4.5B/yr combined procurement spend on infra/services — forms are a tiny slice of this but confirms these are real procuring entities with formal inkoop-/aanbestedingsbeleid) and 12 provincies, all subject to the same accessibility law.
- **Dimpact**: cooperative of >40 municipalities (44 at time of one source) building shared front/mid-office software, co-owner of Open Formulieren's roadmap. https://www.dimpact.nl/over-ons/vereniging/
- PodiumD (Dimpact's open-source service-delivery platform) "formulier" module in production at: Assen, Groningen, Enschede, Gemert-Bakel, Kampen, Oldambt, Oldenzaal, Roermond, Rotterdam, Zwolle. PodiumD "portaal" at: Assen, Enschede, Groningen, Oost Gelre, Zwolle. https://www.dimpact.nl/podiumd-formulier/

## Open Formulieren (Open Forms) — the dominant open-source player

- Initiated ~2020 by **Maykin Media** (Python/Django shop) as part of the "Open Gemeente Initiatief"; made open source and code donated to **Dimpact** after Maykin won a Dimpact tender. Co-developed with Utrecht, Den Haag, SED-organisatie, and various Dimpact municipalities. https://www.maykinmedia.nl/en/portfolio/project/open-formulieren/ , https://open-forms.readthedocs.io/en/2.8.3/introduction/team.html
- **License: EUPL. No license fees ever** — municipalities only pay for support/hosting/hardware. Quote (Maykin site): "You never pay license fees for Open Formulieren. You only pay for support and the use of hardware where the components run." This is the direct commercial threat model any competing paid product must reckon with: the base software is free, money is made purely on implementation/hosting/support.
- **Tech stack** (important for positioning a Vue engine):
  - Backend: **Python 3.12 / Django**.
  - Frontend / form runtime: **JavaScript SDK built on top of formio.js**, with published **React** components (`@open-formulieren/sdk` on npm, github.com/open-formulieren/open-forms-sdk). Docs literally say "the underlying React components are published and can serve as a basis for your own components, or even completely replace them" and are explicitly semi-private API (no back-compat guarantee, breaking changes only on major bumps).
  - Form builder: their own `@open-formulieren/formio-builder` — explicitly **not** a competing form-builder to Form.io's; only implements the subset of Form.io features Open Forms actually uses. There's also `open-formulieren/formio-renderer` — "an alternative formio renderer" (separate repo), suggesting the team itself is dissatisfied enough with formio.js internals to be building an alternative renderer.
  - **No Vue anywhere in this ecosystem** — confirmed via targeted search ("Open Formulieren" Vue.js/Vue3 returns nothing related; only generic Vue form libs like Vueform/FormKit/SurveyJS/formio/vue show up, none tied to Dutch gov). This is a genuine stack gap: the dominant Dutch-gov open-source forms product is React+formio.js on a Python backend, not Vue. Since Open Forms exposes forms via a REST API + JS SDK, a Vue-native renderer that talks to the same backend API is technically plausible and would appeal to any agency/gemeente whose front-end stack is Vue/Nuxt rather than React (common among Dutch web agencies).
- **Integrations**: OIDC, SAML/DigiD, eHerkenning, eIDAS for auth; Open Zaak (and other Common Ground "Open-*" components) and StUF-ZkN / ZGW APIs for submission backends; payment providers (Ogone/Ingenico-style), calendar apps, pre-fill services (BRP, KVK/Handelsregister). Plugin architecture is the core design principle.
- **Governance/funding**: explicit "public code calls for public responsibility" stance — asks public organisations running it in production to financially contribute to a shared maintenance fund ("Broncodebeheer deelnemers"), separate from paying an implementation partner. https://maykin.nl/nl/partners/
- **Implementation partner network** (this is the direct comparable to what the freelancer could become): Maykin's partners page (https://maykin.nl/nl/partners/) lists **4 implementation partners**: Raakvlak, Daadkracht, Macoin, Taxonic. Their stated role: "Implementatie en configuratie van producten," "Begeleiding bij adoptie en gebruik," "Kennis van processen, organisatie en verandermanagement." No public info on requirements/fees to become one — would need direct contact with Maykin.
- **Delta10** is a second, independent managed-hosting/support vendor for the whole Common Ground open-source stack (Atlas, Signalen, OpenStad, Open Formulieren, Open Zaak, Open Notificaties, NLX) — i.e., an existing example of a small agency building a business purely on "we host/manage/support the free open-source gov components." Gemeente Ede was their first Open Formulieren customer. https://www.delta10.nl/solutions/open-formulieren , https://www.delta10.nl/blog/gemeente-ede-eerste-gebruiker-van-open-formulieren-via-delta10 . They maintain their own fork/mirror at github.com/delta10/open-formulieren and have a customer portal (portal.delta10.nl) with docs — i.e., productized managed-service model, exactly the kind of "resellable service around an open engine" the business goal is aiming for, just currently React/Python-based, not Vue.
- **Adoption is still fairly small**: GEMMA Softwarecatalogus listing shows only **6 gemeenten "in productie"** for the specific package version checked at time of research (https://www.softwarecatalogus.nl/pakket/open-formulieren); a related Seneca Formulierenserver listing showed 9 gemeenten for the older commercial product. These numbers are almost certainly undercounts/stale (Dimpact alone lists 10 municipalities live on PodiumD forms, xxllnc partnered with Maykin, Rotterdam has a doorontwikkeling case via Raakvlak: https://www.raakvlakadvies.nl/cases/doorontwikkeling-open-formulieren) but they signal this is still an early-majority-at-best rollout across 342 municipalities, not a saturated market — real room for competing/complementary offerings.
- **Known friction (from GitHub issue titles, `open-formulieren/open-forms` repo, browsed Sept 2026)**: active work on making conditional-logic rules more robust and auditable — "Fix missing logic rule (static) analysis," "Improve command to bulk enable logic evaluation" (migrating a "new logic evaluation" engine across all existing forms, implying an earlier logic engine had correctness bugs), "FE logic: When the value of number field is used to trigger visibility, components are emptied when 'Clear on hide' is checked" (#3964), "Implement price option logic rule action" (#6730), "Research the UI/UX to configure the verzoektype registration in a form" (#6736 — i.e., mapping form fields to backend case-type registration is still UX-unsolved). Overall picture: the **no-code visual logic/rules builder is the most actively-strained part of the product** — non-trivial conditional logic, computed values, and backend-mapping configuration are still rough edges. This is exactly the kind of thing a code-first, typed metadata engine (like vue-dynamic-form's `computedProps`) is comparatively strong at, if positioned as a tool for developers building custom integrations rather than as a no-code citizen-facing form builder.

## Common Ground / architecture context

- **Common Ground**: VNG-driven (since 2018) IT vision for municipalities: separate data from applications, communicate via standardized APIs ("data bij de bron"). 5-layer reference model: data / service (APIs) / integration (landelijke voorzieningen) / process (BPMN) / interaction (citizen/employee-facing apps). https://www.gemmaonline.nl/wiki/Thema-architectuur_Common_Ground
- Open Formulieren is explicitly positioned as one of the flagship Common Ground-aligned open-source "Open-*" components (alongside Open Zaak, Open Klant, Open Notificaties, Open Inwonerportaal — all Maykin-linked).
- **Important nuance found**: an agency blog (Atabix, a *commercial* Common Ground forms vendor competing with Open Formulieren) argues explicitly that "Common Ground-proof" does not require open source — "open standaarden, modulaire architectuur en keuzevrijheid zijn minstens zo belangrijk" (open standards, modular architecture and freedom of choice matter at least as much as open source itself). Their pitch for a commercial/partner SaaS model: no-code so non-technical gemeente staff can build forms themselves, no self-hosting/patching burden, consultants who "think along" during implementation, no vendor lock-in via open APIs. Quote: "Bij Atabix combineer je beide: zelf de regie, met een partner op de achtergrond" (you combine both: retain control yourself, with a partner in the background). https://blog.atabix.nl/open-source-of-partner-formulieren-kiezen-binnen-common-ground — **this framing (control + a partner in the background, open standards without requiring the buyer to self-host open source) is a template for how a Vue-engine-based freelance offering could be pitched to gemeenten too**, i.e. you don't have to compete head-on as "the open source alternative," you can compete as "an implementation/managed partner using open standards."
- Common Ground supplier landscape also includes **Visma Circle's Djuma** (case management + forms + klantportaal + e-signing, full SaaS, cloud-native "next-gen" zaaksysteem; municipalities: Dronten, Vlissingen) — a proprietary, closed competitor to Open Formulieren, not open-source. https://www.vismacircle.nl/software/formulieren

## Commercial "formulierenserver" vendors (pre-Common-Ground generation)

- **Seneca Formulierenserver**: >10-year-old SaaS forms solution for gemeenten, woningcorporaties (housing associations), zorginstellingen (care institutions). Mid/back-office integration, standard plugins for DigiD/eHerkenning/eIDAS/ADFS login, Ogone payment, digital signatures. GEMMA catalog entry shows only "9 gemeenten" in production for that package version (confidential list). https://seneca.nl/oplossingen/formulierenserver , https://www.softwarecatalogus.nl/pakket/seneca-formulierenserver
- **GEMMA eFormulieren** (GemeenteOplossingen / GO.cms vendor): a subscription catalog of dozens of pre-built, standards-compliant (WCAG) forms; fully based on **StUF-EF** standard (StUF = Standaard Uitwisseling Formaat, the long-standing Dutch municipal data-exchange message standard). Each GEMMA e-formulier ships with an **XML message schema (XSD)**. Completed forms arrive as StUF-EF XML or email and are auto-ingested by common zaaksystemen. This is a **direct structural analogue to vue-dynamic-form's XSD-driven metadata model** — the Dutch municipal world already standardizes forms as XSD-typed XML messages via StUF-EF, meaning a library that can *generate forms directly from XSD* has a plausible, already-existing target format to map onto, not a hypothetical one. Pricing: "voordelig abonnement," pick desired number of forms, base package + add-ons; no public price figures found. https://www.gemeenteoplossingen.nl/producten/content_management/gemma_eformulieren/
- **iWriter**: turned out to be a red herring for "forms" — it's a **document-generation/template tool** (Word/Outlook huisstijl templates, correspondence generation, DMS/zaaksysteem integration), not a citizen-facing forms product. Worth knowing so as not to misclassify it later. Note for future phases: relevant to a different angle (document generation / SmartDocuments-style output) rather than form intake. https://www.iwriter.io/
- **JCC**: search did not surface a distinct "JCC gemeente formulieren" product separate from generic hits; likely refers to a call-center/KCC (Klant Contact Centrum) software vendor, not confirmed as a forms-specific competitor. Flagging as unresolved — would need a follow-up search specifically on "JCC Digitaal" or the vendor's own domain if this needs to be pinned down later. Dead end for this session.
- **SmartDocuments**: not independently researched this session beyond the initial search (appeared in query but no dedicated fetch was done) — flagging as a gap; SmartDocuments is generally known as a correspondence/document-generation vendor for gemeenten (similar category to iWriter) rather than a pure forms-intake product, but this should be verified in a follow-up pass if document-generation adjacency becomes relevant.

## Accessibility / legal obligations (WCAG, Besluit digitale toegankelijkheid)

- Legal basis: **Tijdelijk besluit digitale toegankelijkheid overheid** (BWBR0040936), since folded into the **Wet digitale overheid**. Requires WCAG 2.1 level A + AA (50 success criteria) for all government digital channels used for service delivery — explicitly including **forms** ("website, apps en formulieren"). https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0040936/ , https://digitaaltoegankelijk.nl/sectoren/digitale-toegankelijkheid-voor-gemeente-en-overheid/
- All overheid bodies must publish a formal **toegankelijkheidsverklaring** (accessibility statement) in a national register, ideally backed by an independent recent WCAG audit.
- **European Accessibility Act (EAA)** deadline of **June 2025** adds pressure — non-compliant organizations risk complaints, enforcement action, reputational damage (source: general accessibility-audit vendor copy, treat as directionally correct but not a primary legal citation).
- Real-world audit example found: an accessibility audit of noordzeekanaalgebied.nl (Nov 2025) found only 31 of 55 success criteria "sufficient," 24 open issues, explicitly citing **forms** among the failure areas: "insufficient color contrast, missing accessible names, illogical keyboard focus and elements not operable by keyboard." https://audit.properaccess.nl/reports/202511_nzkg/ — concrete evidence that even recent government-adjacent sites still fail WCAG on form-related criteria, i.e. accessible-by-construction form tooling remains a real, currently-unmet need, not a solved problem.
- **NL Design System** (nldesignsystem.nl, developer.overheid.nl-hosted): a cross-government, community-run ("estafette model") design system with explicit form-building guidelines and components, aimed at accessible/inclusive/reusable patterns. It is a *design/pattern* layer, not a form-engine — a Vue template layer implementing NL Design System's form patterns on top of a metadata-driven engine could be a very concrete, narrow productizable offering (matches vue-dynamic-form's "template owns layout/styling via slots" architecture almost exactly). https://nldesignsystem.nl/ , https://developer.overheid.nl/kennisbank/front-end/design-systems/nl-design-system/
- Nationale ombudsman has publicly criticized digital government forms usability (not accessibility per se) — timeouts losing entered data, unclear confirmation of submission, mobile postcode-field bugs, unclear distinction between complaint types. Generic UX complaint piece, useful as color/evidence of citizen-facing pain but not tied to a specific vendor. https://www.nationaleombudsman.nl/nieuws/column/2019/worstelen-met-digitale-formulieren (note: dated 2019, so treat as illustrative rather than current-state evidence).

## Procurement (aanbesteding) process — thin results, flag as gap

- Could not find a specific, current (2025/2026) formal tender ("aanbesteding") or marktconsultatie specifically for a "formulierenapplicatie" on TenderNed via web search — search only surfaced generic procurement-policy documents (inkoop- en aanbestedingsbeleid) for various gemeenten/waterschappen, not an actual forms-specific tender. **This is a gap**: to understand how gemeenten actually procure (single/multi-supplier framework agreements, DAS/dynamic purchasing systems, threshold values, typical contract duration), a direct TenderNed search (tenderned.nl, filtered on category "software" + keyword "formulieren") would be needed in a follow-up pass rather than generic Google-style web search.
- What is established: procurement above EU thresholds must go through formal (often European) tenders; municipalities commonly join shared cooperatives (Dimpact) or shared catalogs (GEMMA Softwarecatalogus, itself a VNG/KING-run reference list used partly to avoid re-tendering commodity software) rather than each running individual tenders for forms software. This shared-procurement pattern (Dimpact, GEMMA catalog) is itself a barrier to entry for a lone freelancer: the practical sales motion is less "win a public tender" and more "become a partner/sub-contractor to an existing shared-service cooperative or to one of the 4 Maykin implementation partners / Delta10-style managed-service shops," or sell directly to smaller gemeenten/waterschappen/provincies outside the big cooperatives who still self-procure bespoke work.

## Direct takeaways for strategy (not exhaustive — see structured output)

1. Free/EUPL-licensed Open Formulieren has structurally killed the market for a paid *forms engine* sold directly to gemeenten — money in this ecosystem is made on implementation, hosting, support and custom integration work, not licenses. Any go-to-market must be a services/implementation play, not a product-license play, exactly matching the stated business goal.
2. The whole ecosystem is Python/Django backend + React/formio.js frontend. No Vue presence found anywhere. A Vue-native alternative frontend/renderer (potentially consuming the same Open Forms REST API, or standing fully apart for non-Open-Forms-backed gemeenten) is a genuine, unclaimed technical niche, particularly attractive to the many Dutch agencies whose default stack is Vue/Nuxt rather than React.
3. StUF-EF / GEMMA eFormulieren already model municipal forms as XSD-typed XML — vue-dynamic-form's "generate forms from XSD" capability maps onto a real, standardized Dutch artifact type, not just an abstract XSD-generation feature.
4. The most-cited friction in Open Forms' own issue tracker is around the no-code conditional-logic/rules builder and mapping form fields to backend case-type registrations — areas where a code-first, typed engine with `computedProps` is architecturally well-suited, but only sellable to developers/implementation partners, not to non-technical gemeente staff directly (Open Forms' own no-code pitch is aimed at the latter).
5. Existing small-agency business models already prove the "resell services around a free open-source gov engine" thesis works at this scale: Delta10 (managed hosting/support across the whole Open-* stack) and the 4 official Maykin implementation partners (Raakvlak, Daadkracht, Macoin, Taxonic) are all doing exactly this, just not Vue-specific. Becoming a 5th implementation partner, or a Delta10-style managed-service shop specifically for teams wanting a Vue frontend, or a specialist sub-contractor to the existing 4 partners for exactly the "complex logic / XSD-driven forms" cases, are all concretely modeled paths.
6. Accessibility (WCAG/EAA) obligations are legally mandatory and demonstrably still failing in practice even in 2025 audits — "accessible by construction" is a legitimate, provable differentiator if the Vue template layer ships an NL-Design-System-compliant reference template out of the box.
7. Adoption of Open Formulieren itself is still modest (single-digit-to-low-teens gemeenten confirmed in production per available catalogs, against 342 total municipalities) — the market is far from saturated; there is real room to compete for the remaining unmigrated majority, including via commercial/partner routes (as Atabix's own positioning argues) rather than only via the open-source route.
