# Verification notes: phase1-lowcode-market-gap claims

## Claim 1 — Formstack no self-hosted option
Searched "Formstack self-hosted on-premise deployment option enterprise", fetched formstack.com/pricing directly.
Formstack pricing page lists Forms, Suite, Salesforce Solutions, Enterprise (custom quote) — all cloud/SaaS, no self-hosted or on-premise deployment mentioned anywhere, including Enterprise. formstack.com/enterprise page (surfaced in search) likewise describes cloud enterprise workflow automation with custom SLAs and dedicated support, not self-hosting. Formbricks blog (the cited source) makes the same claim; corroborated independently on Formstack's own site. Absence claims can't be proven with 100% certainty (a hidden white-glove on-prem deal is conceivable) but nothing in Formstack's public materials, enterprise page, or third-party comparisons indicates self-hosting is offered at any tier.
Verdict: confirmed.

## Claim 2 — Form.io 90% self-hosting stat + $330/month non-usage-based
Fetched form.io/enterprise-self-hosting/ directly: page opens with "90% of our customers opt for enterprise self hosting the Form.io platform in their environment" — confirms the statistic verbatim on Form.io's own site (same source as cited, so this is vendor self-report, not independently corroborated, but the quote is accurate).
Fetched form.io/configuration-based-pricing/: explicitly non-usage-based ("we don't price per form/submission/API call/etc"). Per-environment pricing: API Server Environment $330/month, Enterprise Level Project $330/month (minimum one required), API Plus/PDF Plus $660/month. This corroborates the ~$330/month per environment figure independently of the enterprise-self-hosting page.
Note: the 90% figure itself has no independent (non-Form.io) corroboration found — it's a vendor marketing claim. The pricing figure is corroborated across two of Form.io's own pages plus third-party summaries (SaaSworthy).
Verdict: confirmed for the pricing/non-usage-based part (independently cross-checked across pages); the 90% figure is accurately quoted but remains vendor self-report with no third-party audit found — still counts as confirmed since the claim is about what Form.io *states*, not an independently audited fact, and the claim as written matches the source exactly.

## Claim 3 — Jotform Enterprise gates white-label, SSO, dedicated server behind custom-quote tier
Fetched jotform.com/enterprise/pricing/ and jotform.com/pricing/.
Correction found: white-labeling (removable Jotform branding) is NOT Enterprise-exclusive — it's available starting at the Bronze paid tier (~$39/mo), per Jotform's own pricing comparison table. Custom domains specifically appear at Gold and Enterprise. True "custom domain with no branding" bundle is more nuanced than the claim states.
SSO and dedicated server environments ARE Enterprise-only and require a custom sales quote — this part checks out.
Verdict: refuted (partially) — the claim overstates by lumping "true white-labeling" in with SSO/dedicated servers as all Enterprise-gated; removable branding is available well below Enterprise (Bronze tier), though custom domain and SSO/dedicated infra are indeed Enterprise-gated with custom pricing.

## Claim 4 — Microsoft Forms branching: Choice-only, forward-only, no loops
Searched and fetched Plumsail's guide plus corroborating sources (Microsoft's own support docs referenced in search snippets, itechguides, formlova).
Multiple independent sources agree: branching triggers are Choice-type questions, branching only moves forward (cannot go to earlier questions, no loop/repeat), consistent across Plumsail, itechguides, formlova, and Microsoft's own support article content surfaced in search snippets.
Verdict: confirmed.

## Claim 5 — Typeform & Jotform CLOUD Act exposure despite EU hosting
Searched independently for Typeform GDPR/CLOUD Act/AWS Ireland status (not just the meetergo scan) and for Jotform EU data residency details.
Typeform: independent sources (dpa-atlas.foundagent.net, general AWS GDPR/CLOUD Act commentary) corroborate that Typeform Inc. (US entity) appears in the data-processing chain and that CLOUD Act exposure exists for US-affiliated providers regardless of EU hosting region — consistent with the claim, though one search result suggested Typeform's AWS hosting may not even be EU-only by default (further supporting, not undermining, the sovereignty concern). Note the claim's specific detail "hosted on AWS Ireland" was not independently pinned down to Ireland specifically (some sources say AWS EU without specifying Ireland vs. Frankfurt); this is a minor unverified detail within an otherwise-supported claim.
Jotform: independently confirmed via jotform.com/help articles that (a) Jotform is US-headquartered, (b) standard accounts can opt into EU server storage but full local/EU-only data residency choice is Enterprise-only, (c) EU data (Frankfurt) is hosted via AWS or Google Cloud depending on the source (Jotform's own help articles show mixed AWS/Google Cloud framing for Frankfurt — the "Google Frankfurt" detail in the claim is plausible but not cleanly confirmed; some Jotform docs attribute Frankfurt to AWS, others reference Google Cloud more generally for Iowa US).
The core legal argument (US CLOUD Act reaches EU-hosted data when the parent/processing entity is US-headquartered) is a genuinely contested legal question post-Schrems II, not settled fact — appropriately treated as narrative/analysis rather than verified legal conclusion, matching the claim's own "low confidence" framing.
Verdict: unverified — directionally supported by independent sources on the legal mechanism and on Jotform's Enterprise-gated EU residency, but specific infrastructure details (AWS Ireland vs EU generally; Google vs AWS Frankfurt) are not cleanly pinned down independently, and the ultimate CLOUD Act legal exposure claim is inherently a matter of legal interpretation rather than a verifiable fact.

## Claim 6 — Digipoort SOAP/XSD + free Logius ODB developer access
Fetched logius.nl Digipoort "Aansluiten voor Bedrijven" manual directly and odb.belastingdienst.nl/en/services-from-logius/.
SOAP/XSD part confirmed directly: the Logius manual states webservices exchange SOAP messages whose structure is specified in XSD (referenced from WSDL).
"Free ODB developer account" part NOT confirmed: neither the Logius manual page nor the Belastingdienst ODB services page describes an account-based developer program called "ODB account" granting access to specs. What exists instead: publicly accessible documentation (no login mentioned) plus the "Aansluit Suite Digipoort" (Compliance/Validation Suite) and a separate public validation tool (nlvalidatie.nl / XBRL validation service) usable without describing any registration/account step. "ODB" here appears to refer to "Ondersteuning Digitaal Berichtenverkeer" (Support Digital Messaging), a Belastingdienst support service, not a "developer account" product as the claim frames it.
Verdict: refuted (partially) — SOAP/XSD structure claim confirmed; the specific "free ODB account" mechanism for accessing specs is not substantiated by the primary sources found. Documentation and a validation service do appear to be freely/publicly available, but not packaged as a named "ODB account" for developers as claimed.
