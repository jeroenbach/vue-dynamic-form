# Phase 1 Research: Regulated verticals in EU/NL needing complex intake forms

Angle: insurance (claims, applications, SIVI), healthcare intake (huisartsen, GGZ, ziekenhuizen), legal intake (advocaten, notarissen), financial onboarding/KYC. Who supplies form/intake software, what it costs, buyer complaints, compliance drivers (AVG/GDPR, NEN 7510, WCAG/EAA) that create switching/build pressure.

Date of research: 2026-09-30. 12 searches run, several pages fetched.

## 1. Insurance — SIVI AFS / AFD (Netherlands)

- SIVI (sivi.org) runs the "All Finance Standaard" (AFS), the Dutch insurance-sector data exchange standard. Users: insurers, volmachten (underwriting agents), brokers/advisors (primary chain parties), claims handlers/experts (third parties), and software/service vendors. https://www.sivi.org/sivi-afs/
- **Confirmed technical fit: SIVI's AFD-definitie Standaard is literally XSD-based.** `afdSchema.xsd` is described as "the basis of the AFD-definition Standard"; the mandatory schema set is `afdSchema.xsd` + `afdFormats.xsd` + `afdCodelists.xsd`, and afdSchema.xsd documents message structure, nesting, code-list usage, and entity/attribute descriptions. There's no rigid AFD framework forcing one schema shape — "the structure of the XML Schema is fundamentally determined by the function for which the message is developed" but AFD guidelines apply. Docs: https://www.manula.com/manuals/sivi/afd-definitie-standaard/1/nl/topic/4-2-1-afdschema-xsd-en-sivi-hulpmiddelen , https://www.manula.com/manuals/sivi/afd-definitie-standaard/1/nl/topic/5-1-xml-schema-en-afd , overview PDF: https://www.sivi.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/afd_paper_maart-2018.pdf
  - **This is a strong, concrete match for the library's XSD-driven metadata model.** An SIVI-AFD XSD could plausibly be fed through (or mapped to) the library's metadata tree to auto-generate policy/claim intake forms that are natively compliant with the sector data standard, rather than hand-built. This is a believable wedge product: "SIVI-to-form generator" or "XSD-import for insurance intermediaries."
  - Globally, the equivalent/competing standard is **ACORD** (US-centric but used in EU for Large Commercial & Reinsurance): ACORD also publishes XML/XSD schemas (P&C, Life/Annuity/Health) — https://www.acord.org/standards-architecture/acord-data-standards , schema docs at https://schemas.liquid-technologies.com/Category/Insurance . Same pattern: standards body publishes XSD, vendors build forms/software around it. Confirms this is a known, provenly monetizable niche pattern in insurance software generally, not just a Dutch quirk.

- **NL insurance software vendor landscape** (from Appfront's "9 beste verzekeringssoftware" roundup, https://appfront.nl/beste-verzekeringssoftware-leveranciers ): Appfront, Keylane (Utrecht, SaaS for damage/life/pension core platforms, 750+ employees, active NL/BE/DE/DK/NO/CH), ANVA (Amersfoort/Bergen op Zoom, cloud platform since 1975, serves insurers/volmachten/service providers/intermediaries), CCS (Woerden), Blinqx (Gouda, SaaS for advisors), Netaspect/Certigo (Breda, policy lifecycle mgmt), SiliconCode (Den Haag, low-code customization — worth watching as a possible tech-stack analog/competitor), Online Pro (Nijmegen), A.i. Automatisering (Breda, insurance CRM). No pricing disclosed for any.
  - Article's own "red flags to watch for" when buying insurance software (i.e. buyer pain points the market itself names): unclear implementation/dev process, no demonstrated experience integrating with insurers, software that can't scale/build modularly, insufficient attention to security/GDPR/auditability, and standard packages not fitting non-standard processes (used to argue for custom dev). This is exactly the pitch surface for a metadata-driven forms engine: modular, auditable-by-construction (XSD-derived validation), and reskinnable per customer.
  - **ANVA limitation found (weak signal, one line only):** a case study snippet said a customer "liepen tegen de grenzen van de softwaremogelijkheden van ANVA aan" (ran into ANVA's software limits) — found via Novulo's "beste ANVA alternatieven" blog post, but that URL 404'd on fetch (https://verzekeren.novulo.com/blog/beste-anva-alternatieven — dead end, don't re-try, try a fresh search for the live URL if needed). Novulo itself (verzekeren.novulo.com) looks like a low-code/model-driven Dutch insurance software vendor — worth a deeper look in a later phase, possibly a competitor to watch, not covered further here.
  - Keylane: reviews found were generic marketing-page positive claims (Gartner Peer Insights snippet said "reliable," "world-class for managing policies") — no substantive complaints surfaced; Gartner/G2 pages need an authenticated fetch to read real reviews, didn't do that here (dead end for anonymous WebFetch).

- **Insurance claims forms consumer-facing complaint surface**: schade-formulieren (damage claim forms) are commonly submitted via insurer web portal or app, or as uploaded PDF; third-party tools like schadeformulier.online exist for filling in the (EU-wide) "Europees Schadeformulier" jointly via QR/SMS. Complaints about insurers that don't resolve claims well escalate to **Kifid** (Klachteninstituut Financiële Dienstverlening), a free, binding dispute body, rulings taking 3–8 months. This shows there's an established, well-known escalation path when claims-intake/handling goes wrong — a lever an intake-quality pitch ("get it right the first time, reduce Kifid escalations") could use, but I found no direct quote of a consumer complaining specifically about claim *form* UX (only general claims-process complaints). Source: https://verzekerexpert.nl/blog/schade-claim-indienen/

## 2. Healthcare intake — huisartsen / GGZ / anamnese / ROM

- **Anamnesis / intake questionnaire tooling** is a well-established, competitive sub-market in Dutch healthcare software, not a greenfield need — meaning any play here competes with entrenched, EPD-integrated vendors, not a blank slate:
  - **Embloom** (embloom.nl) — anamnesis/questionnaire platform (Anamnese Vragenlijst "AV", auto-scores answers, generates indications for socio-economic/emotional/behavioural problems); used in huisartsenpraktijk, GGZ, algemeen maatschappelijk werk. Pricing: basic/premium subscription tiers, questionnaire *licence* costs are separate, set by the test publisher (not Embloom) — https://www.embloom.nl/support/wat-zijn-de-kosten-van-embloom/ , https://www.embloom.nl/content/av/
  - **Therapieland** (therapieland.nl) — e-health/GGZ platform with published pricing: €35,75/behandelaar/maand (programme licence), €13,75/behandelaar/maand (vragenlijstenportaal), €12,50/behandelaar/maand (video calling); some questionnaires carry extra per-use cost, billed twice yearly. https://www.therapieland.nl/tarieven
  - **Intramed / Incura / QuestPro / FysioRoadmap** — EPD/ECD vendors for paramedische praktijken (fysio, diëtiek) bundling online intake/anamnese/PREM questionnaires into the patient record system. https://www.intramed.nl/epd-software/ , https://managewarepro.nl/questpro-paramedisch-incura/
  - **ZorgDomein** — the dominant Dutch digital-referral (verwijzen) platform connecting huisartsen to specialists/GGZ; referrals carry structured patient data per the **HASP** guideline and feed straight into the receiving EPD, removing manual re-entry. https://zorgdomein.com/zorgprofessional/ , https://zorgdomein.com/verwijzen-naar-geestelijke-gezondheidszorg/ — this is the likely *integration point* rather than a target to displace: any GGZ/huisarts intake product would need to interoperate with ZorgDomein-fed data, not compete with it directly.
  - **ROM / HoNOS (GGZ outcome monitoring)**: ROM = Routine Outcome Monitoring, repeated structured measurement of treatment effect; HoNOS is a clinician-scored (not patient-scored) observation checklist; HoNOS+ adds extra items. Vendor **VIPLive Monitoring** (NETQ Healthcare) does digital ROM with client/relative self-entry + dashboards. https://www.digivaardigindezorg.nl/610kr9 , https://viplive.nl/viplive-voor-u/ggz/monitoring-ggz , https://www.embloom.nl/content/honos/
  - Takeaway: healthcare questionnaire tooling in NL is fragmented by clinical instrument (each validated questionnaire, e.g. HoNOS, is separately licensed from its test publisher, layered on top of the delivery platform's own subscription) — a structural pattern a metadata-driven forms engine could simplify (one engine, plug in different instrument definitions as metadata) but licensing of the clinical instruments themselves is a separate, non-technical obstacle (publishers gatekeep the actual questionnaire content, e.g. EFP kennisdatabank / Datec for HoNOS: https://kennisdatabank.efp.nl/instrumenten/honos , https://datec.nl/honos/).

- **NEN 7510** (Dutch healthcare information-security standard, ISO 27001/27002 + healthcare-specific controls) applies to any software provider handling patient data, not just care providers themselves — "if a software company manages a system that processes patient data, the requirements apply to them as well." Confirmed via multiple sources, e.g. https://www.nen.nl/zorg-welzijn/ict-in-de-zorg/informatiebeveiliging-in-de-zorg , https://www.computable.nl/topics/nen-7510/ , https://irm360.nl/normen/nen-7510/. Practical implication for a forms *engine* vendor: NEN 7510 is mostly an infosec/ISMS certification burden on whoever *hosts and processes* the data, less a constraint on the client-side form-rendering library itself — relevant only if this library's business model extends into hosting/SaaS around the forms (e.g. an intake-portal product), not if it stays a self-hosted/BYO-backend npm package.
- No direct evidence found of AVG/GDPR-driven complaints specifically about generic form tools (Typeform/Jotform) being unfit for healthcare/insurance in NL — search returned only generic marketing claims that Jotform is "GDPR compliant" and offers HIPAA BAAs; did not find a forum/complaint thread saying "we can't use Typeform because of AVG." This should be treated as an assumption/anecdote in the existing thesis, not yet an externally verified pain point — flag as **not load-bearing without further digging** (a later phase or follow-up search specifically on Dutch privacy-officer forums/LinkedIn might surface it, this session didn't).

## 3. Legal intake — advocaten / notarissen

- Dutch law firms do collect **digital intake forms** before engagement (examples found: El Hannouche, Onland Advocaten, Advocatenkantoor Osté all publish a public intake formulier) — https://www.elhannouche.nl/informatie/intakeformulier/ , https://www.advocatenkantooroste.nl/intakeformulier — these read as simple own-built web forms, not evidence of a shared vertical SaaS product; **JotForm explicitly markets a template for this** ("Digitaal intake-formulier" template, https://www.jotform.com/nl/form-templates/digitaal-intake-formulier ) suggesting many smaller kantoren just use generic form builders today — a real gap/opportunity for a more structured, compliant, schema-driven intake if bigger firms want more rigor.
- **Practice-management suites bundling matter intake**:
  - **Kleos** (Wolters Kluwer) — market-leading web-based practice management in Europe, 30,000+ users; intake is basic (workload/handler visibility), not a dedicated structured-intake engine. https://www.wolterskluwer.com/nl-nl/solutions/legal-software/law-firms , https://www.advocatenblad.nl/advertorials/kleos-de-toekomst-van-legal-tech/
  - **Lexolution** (by Septeo/STP.ONE) — has a proper **New Matter Intake (NMI)** module: "manages entire client acceptance process... from initial contact to intake forms and file creation," standardizes onboarding, runs automated conflict/compliance checks. https://www.stp.one/en/products/lexolution — this is the closest existing analog to a compliance-grade legal intake product; worth studying Lexolution's NMI feature set/pricing in a follow-up as the direct benchmark.
  - **HAMMOCK** (hammock.nl) — Dutch cloud software for advocatenkantoren, document automation + tijdregistratie; intake not confirmed as a distinct module. https://www.hammock.nl/
  - No pricing found for any of Kleos/Lexolution/HAMMOCK (all enterprise "contact us" sales).
- **Notaris / WWFT ("ken uw klant")**: notaries are legal gatekeepers under the **WWFT** (Wet ter voorkoming van Witwassen en Financieren van Terrorisme), obligated to know-your-customer, risk-assess, and report unusual transactions — https://www.tijdschriftfamiliebedrijven.nl/2024/10/02/de-notaris-en-de-wwft-een-match-made-in-heaven/. Could not find a specific vendor/product for digital WWFT-compliant notary intake — this looks like an underserved, well-defined compliance-driven form requirement (structured KYC data capture + risk scoring + audit trail) that maps directly onto this library's strengths (XSD-style validation rules, structured metadata, choice branches for risk categories). **Flagged as a genuine white-space candidate worth a deeper follow-up phase**, not yet verified as underserved beyond "I didn't find a dedicated product," which is a weak negative signal (absence of evidence, not evidence of absence).

## 5. Direct competitor: Form.io

- **Form.io (form.io) is the most directly comparable existing product to this library's positioning** — "enterprise-class combined form and API data management platform for developers building complex form-based business process applications," explicitly serving healthcare, government, banking/finance, insurance, legal, compliance verticals. https://form.io/kyc-onboarding-software-financial-services-form-platforms/
  - Pricing found: Cloud plan $14.99/mo (individuals/small teams, 50 apps, 99.5% uptime), Enterprise $74.95/mo (50 apps, 99.99% uptime, phone support, white-label, dedicated account rep, extra security/compliance), **self-hosted Enterprise from $300/mo**, and fully custom enterprise pricing above that. https://form.io/configuration-based-pricing/ , summarized via lowcode.agency review and Capterra/SaaSWorthy listings.
  - Form.io is JSON-schema-driven (not XSD/XML), API-first, drag-and-drop builder, conditional logic, e-signature, workflow automation, offline access, full-text search, archiving/retention.
  - **Strategic read**: Form.io proves the underlying business model works (developer-facing, schema-driven form engine monetized via self-hosted/enterprise licensing, sold into exactly these regulated verticals) at meaningful scale/price points ($300–unlimited/mo self-hosted, presumably much higher for true enterprise deals). This library's differentiation vs. Form.io would need to be: (a) native XSD/XML-schema fidelity (real fit for SIVI/ACORD-style standards bodies that already publish XSD, vs. Form.io's JSON-first model), (b) the strict engine/template separation enabling per-customer reskinning without touching form logic, (c) Vue-native (Form.io is framework-agnostic/vanilla-JS core with wrappers, could be positioned as heavier for Vue shops), (d) wizard-as-first-class-metadata. This is the single most important competitive reference point found in this research pass — **load-bearing for any go-to-market strategy**.

## 6. Compliance drivers — WCAG / EAA / accessibility (cross-vertical)

- **European Accessibility Act (EAA)** has been in force since **28 June 2025** across all EU member states; in NL, **ACM (Autoriteit Consument & Markt)** is the enforcing supervisor since that date. Max fine: **€900,000 or 4% of annual turnover, whichever is higher**, plus daily penalty payments for continued non-compliance. Source: https://trustyourwebsite.com/nl/nl/guides/eaa-boetes
- Dutch government sites/apps already mandatorily follow **EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 AA** under the "Tijdelijk besluit digitale toegankelijkheid overheid" since 2018, with a published toegankelijkheidsverklaring (accessibility statement) required — a new statement model takes effect **1 October 2026**. Having a statement does *not* shield an organization from a fine if the actual technical requirements aren't met. Source: https://www.digitaleoverheid.nl/nieuws/model-toegankelijkheidsverklaring-vastgesteld/
- **This is a genuinely strong, dated compliance driver applicable directly to forms**: the EAA specifically covers "banking services" and e-commerce among its scoped sectors (private sector, not just government), meaning **insurance and banking forms in the EU are now under a binding, enforced accessibility mandate with large fines**, not just a best-practice recommendation. This significantly strengthens the "compliance creates switching/build pressure" thesis for the insurance/financial verticals specifically. Worth verifying in a follow-up exactly which of this research's four verticals (insurance, healthcare, legal, financial) fall within the EAA's scoped list vs. only under the older government-only decree — this pass confirms government + banking are in scope, did not fully confirm insurance/healthcare/legal private-sector scope, flag as **needs verification**.
